CISA Guidance on Phishing and Social Engineering

Phishing remains one of the most common initial access vectors. Attackers send emails or messages that appear legitimate to trick users into revealing credentials or installing malware.

Common indicators:
- Urgent language requesting immediate action.
- Links that do not match the displayed text or lead to unexpected domains.
- Attachments with macros or unexpected file types.
- Requests for credentials, MFA codes, or financial information.
- Spoofed sender addresses that closely resemble trusted organizations.

Recommended defenses:
- Train users to hover over links and verify sender addresses.
- Deploy email filtering and attachment sandboxing.
- Enable multi-factor authentication so stolen passwords alone are insufficient.
- Implement DMARC, DKIM, and SPF for outbound and inbound email authentication.
- Establish a clear reporting process for suspicious messages.
- Use endpoint detection to catch malware that bypasses email filters.

Organizations should assume some phishing emails will reach users and design controls and training accordingly. Report confirmed phishing campaigns to CISA and relevant ISACs.
